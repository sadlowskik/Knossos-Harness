# Living Rome — Production Art Bible

## Purpose

Living Rome renders real agent operations as a developing Roman campaign world. Rome is the visual language, never the data model: project, objective, agent, tool, service, website, dependency, failure, and verification remain literal product concepts.

## Visual register

- Illustrated strategy-game assets viewed from a high three-quarter isometric angle.
- Stylized 2D/2.5D painting with crisp silhouettes and restrained dimensionality.
- More authored and tactile than vector UI; less realistic than architectural visualization.
- Mediterranean materials: warm limestone, muted terracotta, aged bronze, dark cypress, canvas, timber, and dusty earth.
- Restrained palette: limestone ivory, Pompeian red, umber, oxidized bronze, olive, Mediterranean blue, and charcoal.
- Fine painted texture that remains legible when reduced; major shapes must read before surface detail.
- Soft lower-right cast shadow and warm upper-left light, consistent across every cutout.
- No text, labels, logos, modern objects, circular icon plates, glossy mobile-game plastic, high fantasy, Vatican imagery, or photorealism.

## Camera and footprint

- Every world asset uses the same elevated three-quarter camera, approximately 35–40 degrees downward.
- Buildings face slightly toward the lower-right.
- Transparent cutouts include generous breathing room for shadows and effects.
- Capital assets are authored around a 4:3 footprint and display at roughly 160–220 CSS pixels.
- Camps display at 110–150 CSS pixels.
- Landmarks and outposts display at 48–88 CSS pixels.
- Agent standards and tool equipment display at 20–36 CSS pixels.

## Semantic families

### Project settlements

Four development tiers using the same underlying settlement plan:

1. Surveyed ground: road marker, foundations, tents, and material stacks.
2. Working settlement: forum foundation, workshop, archive, and connecting road.
3. Established capital: forum, basilica-like hall, workshops, walls, gardens, and active civic detail.
4. Verified capital: completed civic complex, stronger walls, monuments, roads, and subtle olive/bronze verification accents.

### Operational camps

- Forming: sparse tents, rolled standards, planning table.
- Active: occupied command tent, standing standards, tool racks, lit brazier.
- Contested: red and blue standards on opposing edges, disturbed earth, defensive stakes, no gore.
- Blocked: stalled construction, lowered standard, warning brazier/smoke.
- Verified: packed orderly camp, raised verification standard, completed road leading home.

### Infrastructure outposts

- Model endpoint: signal tower and covered computation pavilion.
- Website: coastal beacon or diplomatic gate.
- Service/runtime: fortified workshop with active furnace and dispatch yard.
- Database/storage: masonry granary or archive vault.
- Repository/folder landmark: compact civic workshop/archive/yard chosen by real resource type.

## State language

- Active: warm lamps, raised standard, subtle movement cue.
- Red team: muted Pompeian red cloth and shield accents.
- Blue team: desaturated Mediterranean blue cloth and shield accents.
- Verified: olive-green enamel, orderly masonry, clean road connection, restrained glow only.
- Blocked/error: ember-red warning brazier, smoke, disrupted materials; never fantasy fire magic.
- Growth: additional real architecture and civic activity, not a larger glow.

## Personalization

- Project identity occupies replaceable shields, banners, mosaic panels, and standards.
- The structure and operational meaning remain person-agnostic.
- User color and emblems may tint heraldic surfaces only; stone, terrain, and lighting stay consistent.
